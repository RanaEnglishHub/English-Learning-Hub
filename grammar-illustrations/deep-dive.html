1. Active and Passive Voice

Active: The subject does the action. Passive: The subject receives the action. Use the passive when the action/result matters more than the person doing it, or the doer is unknown.

Formula: object + appropriate form of be + past participle (V3) (+ by + agent).

Tense / structureActivePassive
Present simpleThe team cleans the beach.The beach is cleaned by the team.
Present continuousThey are repairing the road.The road is being repaired.
Present perfectThey have finished the project.The project has been finished.
Past simpleThe students wrote the report.The report was written by the students.
Past continuousThey were painting the wall.The wall was being painted.
Past perfectThey had sent the email.The email had been sent.
Future with willThey will announce the results.The results will be announced.
Going toThey are going to build a library.A library is going to be built.
Modal verbStudents must submit the task.The task must be submitted.
Modal perfectThey should have checked the data.The data should have been checked.
InfinitiveWe need to complete the form.The form needs to be completed.
GerundI dislike people interrupting me.I dislike being interrupted.

Transformations step by step

  1. Find the object: The volunteers planted trees.
  2. Move it to subject position: Trees...
  3. Keep the original tense: past simple → were.
  4. Add V3: Trees were planted.
  5. Add by the volunteers only if important.

Questions, negatives and two objects

Negative: The documents were not signed. Question: Were the documents signed? Two objects: They gave Sara a prize → Sara was given a prize / A prize was given to Sara.

Watch out: Intransitive verbs such as arrive, happen, sleep, die normally have no passive form. Avoid unnecessary by phrases. Rare continuous perfect passives (e.g. has been being repaired) are grammatically possible but usually avoided in everyday writing.

2. Direct and Reported Speech

Direct: Sara said, “I am preparing a presentation.” Reported: Sara said (that) she was preparing a presentation. When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense often shifts back.

Direct speechTypical reported formExample
Present simplePast simple“I work hard.” → She said she worked hard.
Present continuousPast continuous“I am studying.” → She said she was studying.
Present perfectPast perfect“I have finished.” → She said she had finished.
Past simplePast perfect“I saw it.” → She said she had seen it.
Past continuousPast perfect continuous“I was waiting.” → She said she had been waiting.
Will / can / mayWould / could / might“I will help.” → She said she would help.
Must (obligation)Often had to“I must leave.” → She said she had to leave.

Statements, questions, requests and suggestions

Statement: She said (that) she was ready. Yes/no question: “Are you ready?” → She asked if/whether I was ready. Wh-question: “Where do you live?” → She asked where I lived (statement word order; no do). Command: “Close the door.” → She told me to close the door. Negative request: “Don’t be late.” → She asked me not to be late. Suggestion: “Let’s revise.” → She suggested revising / suggested that we revise.

Important: Backshift is not always necessary when the information is still true, or when the reporting verb is present. She says she lives in Fujairah. Modal verbs such as could, would, should, might often remain unchanged. Pronouns and time words change only when the context requires it.

3. Time Expressions and Tense Clues

Signal words are useful clues, not automatic rules. Check meaning, time reference, duration and whether the action is complete.

TenseTypical cluesExample
Present simplealways, usually, often, sometimes, every FridayShe usually revises after school.
Present continuousnow, right now, at the moment, currently, these daysThey are working on the project now.
Present perfectjust, already, yet, ever, never, so far, since, forI have already submitted the task.
Present perfect continuousfor, since, all morning, lately, recentlyShe has been studying for two hours.
Past simpleyesterday, last year, ago, in 2021We visited the museum last week.
Past continuouswhile, as, at 7 p.m. yesterday, whenI was reading when she called.
Past perfectbefore, after, by the time, alreadyThe lesson had started before we arrived.
Past perfect continuousfor, since, before, until thenThey had been practising for weeks before the show.
Future with willtomorrow, next week, soon, in the futureI will call tomorrow.
Future continuousthis time tomorrow, at 9 a.m. next MondayThis time tomorrow, I will be travelling.
Future perfectby Friday, by then, by the timeBy Friday, we will have finished.
Future perfect continuousfor + duration + by a future pointBy June, she will have been teaching for ten years.

Compare the meaning

I have lived here for five years (a continuing situation) vs I lived here for five years (a finished period). When I arrived, she cooked (then she cooked) vs When I arrived, she was cooking (action in progress). By Friday, I will finish (prediction) vs By Friday, I will have finished (completion before a deadline).

Word position: Frequency adverbs usually come before the main verb (She often reads) but after be (She is always helpful). Yet often appears at the end of negatives and questions. Since marks a starting point; for marks a duration.
