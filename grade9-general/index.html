Grammar Lab

Adverbs of degree

Use very, quite, too and enough to show degree. Too means more than is suitable; enough means sufficient. Enough follows an adjective.

Example: The street is very quiet. The bag is too heavy. The room is big enough.

Dependent prepositions

Learn the preposition with the expression: interested in, good at, ready for, worried about.

Example: I am interested in science. She is good at organising her notes.

Present modals

Use should for advice, must for strong obligation, can for ability or permission. Put the base verb after a modal.

Example: You should revise regularly. You must keep your receipt.

Verb forms

Want, hope and plan are followed by to + base verb. Enjoy and avoid are followed by -ing. Can is followed by the base verb without to.

Example: I hope to study medicine. I enjoy reading. I can help.

Prepositions of place

Use next to for beside, opposite for facing, between for a position with one thing on either side, and in front of for ahead of.

Example: The library is between the bank and the café.

Indirect questions

Start politely with Could you tell me…? Then use statement word order. Use if or whether for a yes/no question.

Example: Where is the library? → Could you tell me where the library is?

Future: going to

Use am/is/are + going to + base verb for plans and predictions based on present evidence.

Example: We are going to compare prices. Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.

Coordinating conjunctions

And adds information; but shows contrast; or offers a choice; so introduces a result. Join complete clauses clearly.

Example: The shop was closed, so we went home.

Adverbial linkers

However introduces contrast. Therefore introduces a result. For example introduces an example. Use suitable punctuation.

Example: The bag was expensive. However, it lasted for years.

Practice A · Returns and refunds

The Returns Desk at Green Mall The returns desk at Green Mall is on the ground floor, opposite the main entrance. A clear sign above it says Customer Care. The desk has two queues: one for exchanges and one for refunds. This helps customers choose the right place before they speak to a member of staff. On Saturday, Mariam brought a blue jacket to the desk because its zip would not close. She bought it three days earlier and still had the receipt. She wanted the same jacket in the same size, but every replacement was sold out. The assistant therefore offered her a full refund. Because Mariam had paid by card, the money would return to that card within five working days. Nearby, Hessa wanted to return a green sweater simply because she preferred another colour. The sweater was unused and its label was attached. Under the shop’s policy, customers who change their minds can exchange an unused item within fourteen days if they bring proof of purchase. Hessa selected a yellow sweater at the same price, so she did not pay extra. A notice explains that damaged items and changes of mind are handled differently. Customers should read the policy and keep receipts. If an item was bought online, the desk can check the order number instead. Staff ask customers to explain the problem politely so they can find a suitable solution.

Practice B · Success in college

Building Habits for College Success Starting college brings greater freedom, but it also requires students to manage their own learning. A successful routine is not simply a long list of tasks. It is a realistic plan that leaves time for classes, independent study and rest. Students who understand their responsibilities can make better decisions about how to spend each day. One useful approach is to divide large assignments into smaller steps. Instead of trying to write a whole report in one evening, a student can collect information on Monday, organise ideas on Tuesday and draft the report later. Short deadlines make progress easier to notice. They also leave time to ask questions before the final submission date. College facilities can support this process. The library offers a quiet place to read, while a tutor can explain a difficult concept. A study group provides another kind of support: members exchange ideas and explain answers to one another. However, a group becomes less useful if its members only chat or copy work. Each person should prepare before the meeting and contribute independently. Rest is equally important. A student who studies late every night may feel tired in morning classes and miss important information. Regular breaks and enough sleep help students return to their work with more attention. Success therefore depends on balance rather than on studying without stopping. By reviewing their habits each week, students can keep strategies that work and change those that do not.

Writing Studio

Build a clear paragraph

Start with a topic sentence. Add details and a reason or example. Finish with a useful final thought. Example: A regular study routine helps me learn. I review my notes after school and write down questions for my teacher. This helps me notice what I do not understand. A short review each day is easier than leaving everything until the test.

Summarise without copying

Identify who is writing, the main topic and the key message. Leave out minor examples. Change the wording while keeping the meaning. Do not add your own opinion.

Write an informal reply

Use a greeting, respond to the sender, cover all three guiding points in clear paragraphs, give reasons and examples, and finish with a suitable closing. Check spelling, punctuation and verb forms. A separate writing plan is not an exam submission requirement.

We also saw the sports hall and computer rooms. These (4) ___ are available to all students after registration. Before leaving, we checked a street map and chose our (5) ___ back to school. We decided to take the road beside the park because it had safe crossings and less traffic. On the bus, we discussed how a neighbourhood can support learning. Some students preferred living near shops, while others wanted a peaceful place to study. Everyone agreed that clear information about transport and local services would help a new student settle into the area and feel confident on the first day.

Part 4 · Writing /40

Hi Aisha, I have just finished my first week at college, and I wanted to tell you about it. At first, I felt nervous because the campus seemed so large. Fortunately, a student guide showed me the library, the health centre and the rooms where I can meet a tutor. My favourite place is the library because it is quiet and has comfortable seats. I usually go there after class to review my notes. I have also joined a study group. We meet on Tuesdays and help one another understand difficult topics, but we always write our own assignments. Getting around was confusing on my first day. Now I use a campus map, and I know a short route from my classroom to the bus stop. Next week, I am going to join the reading club. How do you organise your studies, and what advice would you give a new student? Write soon! Best wishes, Maha

Example summary

Maha describes her first week at college, including useful campus facilities and her new study routine. Although she felt nervous and found directions confusing at first, she now feels more confident and asks Aisha for study advice.

Example reply

Hi Maha, Thank you for your email. I am glad you are feeling more confident at college. The library sounds like a comfortable place to study, and your reading club sounds interesting. I organise my studies with a weekly timetable. After school, I check my assignments and divide large tasks into smaller steps. I usually revise the most difficult subject first because I have more energy then. I also leave time for breaks and family activities. Last year, I joined a new study group and felt shy at first. Preparing a few questions before each meeting helped me take part. I think you should keep using your campus map and ask a tutor whenever a topic is unclear. You should also get enough sleep instead of studying late every night. This will help you concentrate in class. Tell me how your first reading club meeting goes! Best wishes, Aisha

Other relevant answers are possible. Ask your teacher to review your writing; this page does not award an automatic writing mark.
