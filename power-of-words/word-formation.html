1. How word families work

A word family shares a root but changes grammatical form. The correct form depends on the sentence: a noun names a concept, a verb expresses an action, an adjective describes a noun, and an adverb usually modifies a verb or adjective.

VerbNounAdjectiveAdverb
educateeducationeducationaleducationally
createcreation / creativitycreativecreatively
decidedecisiondecisivedecisively
protectprotectionprotectiveprotectively
succeedsuccesssuccessfulsuccessfully
analyseanalysisanalyticalanalytically

Notice: Some forms change spelling (decide → decision; analyse → analysis), and not every family has a common form in every column.

2. Prefixes change meaning

un-/in-/im-/ir-/il-/dis- often form opposites: unfair, incomplete, impossible, irregular, illegal, dishonest. re- means again (rewrite); mis- means incorrectly (misunderstand); pre- means before (preview).

Spelling tip: im- commonly appears before p/m (impossible); ir- before r (irregular); il- before l (illegal). Not every prefix works with every word.

3. Suffixes change word class

FormCommon endingsExamples
Noun-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -erinnovation, development, kindness, responsibility, researcher
Adjective-ful, -less, -ive, -al, -ablehelpful, careless, effective, cultural, sustainable
Adverb-lycarefully, independently
Verb-ise/-ize, -enorganise, strengthen

Exam strategy: Read the words before and after the gap. After an article you may need a noun; before a noun you often need an adjective; after a linking verb you often need an adjective.
